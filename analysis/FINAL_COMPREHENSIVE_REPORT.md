## 3. Convergent Validity (Guna vs. Big Five)
We tested the hypothesis that Ancient Gunas map to modern Western traits. The results were visibly striking.

### Correlation Heatmap
![Correlation Heatmap](file:///c:/Users/donke/Desktop/IKS_Work/Gunabased%20Survey/student-assessment/analysis/phase3_correlation_heatmap.png)

### Key Findings
1.  **Sattva $\approx$ Conscientiousness ($r=0.61$)**: confirming Sattva as the trait of duty, organization, and discipline.
2.  **Tamas $\approx$ Neuroticism ($r=0.65$)**: confirming Tamas as the trait of emotional instability and anxiety.
3.  **Tamas $\neq$ Conscientiousness ($r=-0.47$)**: Tamas is strongly *inversely* related to discipline, distinguishing it as active "neglect" rather than just neutrality.
4.  **Rajas**: Maps to both **Neuroticism** ($r=0.50$) and **Extraversion** ($r=0.28$), portraying the "Active but Anxious" nature (Type A personality).

## 4. Student Insights (Hypothesis Testing)
We explored how these traits play out in the actual student population.

### Gender Differences
**Result: No Significant Difference.**
Males and Females scored similarly on all three Gunas ($p > 0.27$). This suggests the GPI is a gender-neutral tool suitable for broad application.

### The "Maturation Effect" (Year of Study)
We observed **statistically significant** trends across the 4 years of undergraduate study.

1.  **Sattva Increases ($p=0.042$)**: Students become more balanced and disciplined.
2.  **Rajas Decreases ($p=0.048$)**: High agitation/passion tends to moderate over time.

![Sattva Year Trend](file:///c:/Users/donke/Desktop/IKS_Work/Gunabased%20Survey/student-assessment/analysis/phase4_year_sattva.png)

*Interpretation*: As students progress through the university system, they appear to develop higher levels of self-regulation (Sattva) and reduced agitation (Rajas), likely adapting to the demands of academic life.

## 5. Conclusion & Recommendations
The expanded dataset ($N=50$) has provided even stronger validation for the GPI.

**Recommendations**:
1.  **Publication**: The high Construct Validity ($r > 0.60$) and significant ANOVA results ($p < 0.05$) form a solid basis for a research paper on "Cross-Cultural Personality Assessment".
2.  **Intervention**: The strong link between Tamas and Neuroticism implies that tracking Tamas scores could be an early warning system for student mental health.
3.  **Longitudinal Study**: tracking the "Maturation Effect" by following the same students from Year 1 to Year 4 would be a valuable follow-up study.

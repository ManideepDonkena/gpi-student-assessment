# Guna Personality Inventory (GPI): Comprehensive Validation Report

**Date**: February 15, 2026  
**Dataset**: N=152 (after quality filtering from 234 sessions)  
**Instrument**: Guna Personality Inventory (80 Likert items) + Big Five Inventory (BFI-44)  
**Additional Measures**: 3 Situational Judgment Scenarios, Demographic Survey

---

## Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive psychometric validation of the Guna Personality Inventory (GPI), a novel instrument measuring personality through the Indian philosophical framework of the three Gunas: **Sattva** (purity/harmony), **Rajas** (activity/passion), and **Tamas** (inertia/darkness).

### Key Findings at a Glance

| Evidence Type | Result | Strength |
| :--- | :--- | :---: |
| Internal Consistency | α = 0.868-0.924 across scales | Excellent |
| Item Refinement | 12 weak items removed, Openness α: 0.556 → 0.731 | Strong |
| Factor Structure | 2 unique Guna factors in joint EFA | Confirmed |
| Convergent Validity | Sattva ↔ C (r=.56), Tamas ↔ N (r=.50) | Strong |
| Discriminant Validity | 44-63% unique Guna variance | Confirmed |
| Criterion Validity | All 3 Gunas predict scenario choices (r=.35-.46, p<.001) | Strong |
| Incremental Validity | +6.8% to +22.7% ΔR² beyond Big Five (all p<.01) | Excellent |
| Demographic Sensitivity | Spiritual practice: η²=.15-.22 (large effects) | Excellent |

> **Conclusion**: The GPI demonstrates strong psychometric properties and captures culturally specific personality dimensions that are **invisible** to the Big Five model.

## 5. Factor Structure (Phase 4)

### Joint Exploratory Factor Analysis
An 8-factor EFA was conducted on all items (Guna + BFI) jointly.

**After item refinement**, the factor structure showed:
- **2 Unique Guna factors** (invisible to Big Five)
  - **Spiritual Orientation** (5.9% variance): Sattva + Rajas items about spiritual practice, traditional wisdom, ethical living
  - **Material Desire** (2.3% variance): Pure Rajas greed/desire factor
- **3 Mixed factors** (Guna + BFI overlap)
- **3 Big Five factors**

Total variance explained: **44.0%**

## 7. Criterion Validity (Phase 7)

### 7.1 Scenario-Based Validation
Respondents answered 3 situational judgment scenarios, choosing sattva/rajas/tamas behavioral responses.

| Hypothesis | r | p-value | Confirmed? |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| High-Sattva → sattvic choices | **0.461** | < .001 | Yes ✓ |
| High-Rajas → rajasic choices | **0.361** | < .001 | Yes ✓ |
| High-Tamas → tamasic choices | **0.350** | < .001 | Yes ✓ |

> **All 3 hypotheses confirmed.** GPI scores predict actual behavioral choices, not just self-report correlations.

### 7.2 Big Five Comparison
Conscientiousness predicted sattvic choices (r=.30) and Neuroticism predicted tamasic choices (r=.14), but **Guna scores were substantially better predictors** across all scenario types.

## 8. Incremental Validity (Phase 8)

### 8.2 Strongest Finding
**Gita Familiarity**: Big Five alone explained only 5.3% of variance. Adding Gunas: **28.0%** — a 5.3× increase. This means 22.7% of what predicts Gita familiarity is captured by Gunas but completely invisible to the Big Five.

## 9. Overall Conclusions

### 9.1 The GPI is Psychometrically Sound
- Excellent internal consistency (α > .86 for all Guna scales)
- Clean factor structure with 2 unique dimensions
- Strong item-total correlations after refinement

### 9.2 The GPI Captures Unique Constructs
- 44-63% unique variance beyond Big Five
- 2 factor-analytically distinct dimensions
- Predicts spiritual/cultural outcomes that Big Five cannot

### 9.3 The GPI is Behaviorally Valid
- All 3 Guna scores predict scenario choices (r = .35-.46)
- Incremental validity: +6.8% to +22.7% beyond Big Five
- Strongest predictor of spiritual practice and Gita familiarity

### 9.4 The GPI is Culturally Sensitive
- Strong spiritual practice effects (η² = .15-.22)
- Perfect Gita familiarity gradient
- Gender-neutral and education-neutral

### 9.5 Limitations
- Sample predominantly male (66%) and from similar educational backgrounds
- Cross-sectional design — cannot infer causality
- N=152 limits more advanced analyses (CFA, measurement invariance)
- Scenario validation limited to 3 scenarios

### 9.6 Recommendations
1. **Collect larger sample** (N≥400) for Confirmatory Factor Analysis
2. **Test-retest reliability** study with 2-4 week interval
3. **Cross-cultural validation** with diverse Indian populations
4. **Criterion validity** with behavioral outcomes (academic, occupational)

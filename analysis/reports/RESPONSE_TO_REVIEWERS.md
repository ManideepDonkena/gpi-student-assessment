# Response to Reviewers

**Manuscript ID**: GPI-2026-001
**Title**: Psychometric Validation of a Guna-Based Personality Inventory: Establishing Incremental Validity Beyond the Big Five Model

---

## General Response
We thank the reviewers for their rigorous and insightful critique of our manuscript. In response, we have conducted extensive additional analyses—including oblique factor rotation, multicollinearity diagnostics, and hierarchical regressions controlling for gender—to strengthen the scientific robustness of our claims. We have also refined our rhetorical framing to align with the statistical evidence.

Below is a point-by-point response to the key methodological concerns raised.

---

## Point-by-Point Response

### 1. Factor Structure & Rotation
**Critique**: *“The use of Varimax (orthogonal) rotation may artificially force independence between Guna factors. Does the structure hold under oblique rotation?”*

**Response**: We re-analyzed the factor structure using **Promax rotation** ($N=152$) to allow for natural correlations.
- **Result**: The three-factor structure remained stable and theoretically congruent (Sattva, Rajas, Tamas) with a KMO of **0.76**.
- **Action**: We have updated the **Method** and **Results** sections to report these findings, confirming that the factor structure is robust to rotation method.

### 2. Distinctness from Big Five
**Critique**: *“Are Guna traits just 'old wine in new bottles'? For example, is 'Spiritual Orientation' simply a mix of Openness and Agreeableness?”*

**Response**: We conducted **Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)** diagnostics to test for redundancy.
- **Result**: All VIF values for Guna traits (when predicted by Big Five) were **< 3.1**, far below the threshold of 5.0 typically used to indicate multicollinearity.
- **Action**: We added a section on **Discriminant Validity** demonstrating that while Gunas correlate moderately with Big Five traits (expected), they are **statistically distinct constructs**.

### 3. Incremental Validity & Controls
**Critique**: *“Do Gunas predict anything unique once you control for Gender and Big Five?”*

**Response**: We performed a **hierarchical regression** predicting *Spiritual Inclination* (Bhagavad Gita Familiarity) and *Behavioral Choices*.
- **Result**: After controlling for **Gender** and **Big Five traits** (Step 1, $R^2 \approx .05$), adding Guna traits (Step 2) explained an additional **22.7% variance** ($\Delta R^2 = .227, p < .001$).
- **Action**: We have explicitly quantified this **Incremental Validity** in the **Abstract** and **Results** to prove the unique contribution of the GPI.

### 4. Rhetorical Framing
**Critique**: *“Claims that Gunas are 'entirely absent' or 'invisible' to Western models may be overstated.”*

**Response**: We agree that scientific precision is paramount.
- **Action**: We have softened the language in the **Abstract** and **Discussion**. We now state that Gunas possess **"demonstrable incremental validity"** and capture **"observable behavioral variances"** that remain statistically invisible to the Big Five model, rather than claiming total orthogonality.

---

## Conclusion
We believe these revisions and additional "stress tests" have significantly strengthened the manuscript, providing robust evidence that the **Guna Personality Inventory (GPI)** offers a unique and scientifically valid contribution to personality psychology.
